# Finite-support arithmetic

The low-level norm-fibre API introduced in v0.2 deliberately carries no relative
descent-completeness flag. The v0.3 layer performs the additional quotient,
orientation and local steps described in [relative-descent.md](relative-descent.md).

The additive [quartic etale backend](etale-arithmetic.md) now supplies CRT maps
and factorwise square classes for reducible quartics as v0.4 groundwork. It does
not by itself imply descent completeness. The subsequent
[two-quadratic step](two-quadratic-descent.md) integrates type `(2,2)` into the
complete API; rational-root cases are still unsupported.

## What is computed

For an irreducible squarefree quartic `f` over Q, put `K=Q(theta)` with
`f(theta)=0`. Write `a=lc(f)`. For a finite set of rational primes `S`, the Sage
adapter includes **every** prime ideal over every prime in `S` and computes

\[
 K(S,2)=\{[b]\in K^*/K^{*2}:v_{\mathfrak p}(b)\equiv0\pmod2
                    \text{ for }\mathfrak p\nmid S\}.
\]

This finite F2-vector space includes a class-group 2-torsion contribution;
it is not generally generated by S-units alone. The adapter uses Sage's
`selmer_space(S, 2, proof=...)`, retains its coordinate maps, and checks basis
round trips. The regression over Q(sqrt(-5)) explicitly detects the nonunit
class represented by 2 when S is empty.

The geometric polynomial `f` is retained unchanged. Only the polynomial
presenting the number field is divided by its leading coefficient.

The core computes the affine fibre

\[
 \{[\xi]\in K(S,2):[N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\xi)]=[a]
                     \text{ in }\mathbb Q^*/\mathbb Q^{*2}\}.
\]

Sign and valuation parity are encoded as bits. Gaussian elimination returns
a particular vector and a kernel basis, or proves that this finite fibre is
empty. Each materialized candidate includes an exact witness `r` satisfying
`N(xi)=a*r^2`. The norm is recomputed from the multiplication matrix, not taken
on trust from the linear-algebra calculation.

`limit` bounds materialization, not the size of the computed affine space.
`total_count` records the full fibre size and `norm_fibre_exhausted` records
whether all of it was visited. These properties say **nothing** about
covering equivalence or complete relative descent.

## Conservative support and its necessary-condition lemma

The default support is the union of primes dividing 2, the numerator or
denominator of `a` and `disc(f)`, and the coefficient denominators. Outside S,
`f/a` is monic integral with unit discriminant and its order is etale over Z_p.
This is conservative: avoiding extraneous primes is an optimization, not a
requirement of v0.2.

Here is the precise limited completeness statement behind this choice.
For the square-class construction of `docs/algorithm.md`, suppose a model
`D_xi` is smooth and locally soluble everywhere. Then the class of `xi` modulo
`K^{*2} Q^*` has a representative in `K(S,2)`.

To see the necessary local condition, choose a point over Q_p away from the
finite exceptional fibres where the norm or the quartic affine expression
vanishes. A smooth local curve with a point permits such a choice. At a prime
outside S its image gives, in the etale algebra,

\[
 \xi U^2=c(X-\theta Z),\qquad \min(v_p(X),v_p(Z))=0.
\]

If `Z` is not a unit, `X-theta Z` is a unit in every factor. If `Z` is a unit,
at most one prime ideal can divide it: the relevant factor modulo p is the
single linear factor with root `X/Z`. That prime has residue degree one.
The equation `F(X,Z)=Y^2` and the unit leading coefficient imply that its
valuation is even. Since the algebra is unramified at p, the parities of
`v_P(xi)` are therefore all the same, namely `v_p(c)` modulo 2. Multiplication
by a rational p changes all these parities simultaneously. Only finitely
many primes occur in the divisor of `xi`, so a rational scalar makes all
valuations outside S even.

This proves **containment up to rational scalars**, not a bijection of covering
classes. The low-level foundation pipeline does not perform the scalar quotient, resolve
norm-witness orientations or automorphism identifications, or prove the
completeness of a relative Selmer-set parametrization. Consequently the
pipeline always has `relative_descent_complete=False`. Even a completely
screened fibre is not advertised as the full 4-Selmer group.

A user-supplied smaller S computes exactly that specified `K(S,2)` and norm
fibre. It does not inherit the default support lemma's hypotheses. An odd
norm valuation outside S raises an error rather than silently enlarging S.

## Proof and dependency handling

`proof=True` is passed explicitly to Sage, independently of global Sage proof
settings. Its certified class-group/unit computations are a trusted arithmetic
dependency, not a new small independent certificate supplied by this package.
`proof=False` carries an explicit GRH assumption through the returned batch.
Local point/obstruction certificates are exact and can be checked regardless
of how a candidate was discovered.

Directly constructing `FiniteNormSpace` with generators does not prove that
they are independent or span `K(S,2)`; its default proof status is unverified.
A manually altered status flag cannot turn an unchecked basis into a proof.

Rational square-class encoding strips S-valuations and checks that the residual
numerator and denominator are integer squares. Factorization is needed for
support construction, not for every norm bit vector. Factors are reconstructed
and primality-checked. Large integers use Sage/PARI factorization with
`algorithm="pari", proof=True` when Sage is available; this does not depend
on accepting probable-prime factors from the Python backend. Below `2**64`
the reference layer uses SymPy's documented deterministic
primality range. Larger factors use Sage/PARI `is_prime(proof=True)`, or raise
when Sage is unavailable. A probable-prime result is never labelled certified.

## PARI adapter

`pari_two_covers(ainvs)` imports the basis returned by PARI's `ell2cover`,
including maps to the specified elliptic curve. SymPy independently checks the
Weierstrass equation modulo `y^2-f(x)` and excludes identically zero map
denominators. This is a map-identity check, **not** verification of the covering
degree or certification that the entire PARI basis is complete. The basis is
not the list of all 2-Selmer classes. Cubic polynomials are retained as such;
the quartic constructor does not silently reinterpret them.
